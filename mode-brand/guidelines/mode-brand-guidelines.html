The idea

Every letter of MODE is a coffee.

The mark borrows its grammar from Albert Hollenstein's L'Echoppe monogram: a square, a half-disc and a quarter-disc, all cut from one radius, separated by a thin cut, and finished by hairlines of the same radius. Nothing else is allowed in.

With that kit, each letter of MODE becomes a coffee and still reads as its letter. The m is a square of coffee that drips. The o is half poured, from the side. The D is a cup on its saucer. The e is Hollenstein's own e, left open: the one letter that isn't a drink.

Stacked MO over DE, the four make a compact block that sits in a corner of a sachet the way a seal sits on a letter. The mark never changes: the five modes (Wake, Active, Zen, Vital, Calm) are named on the pack, never drawn into the letters.

The mark

One mark, no separate wordmark. The stack is the logo. It reads in two rows, MO then DE, and is always used whole.

Construction

One radius, one cut, one hairline.

Each letter is 100 units tall. Everything else follows from that.

Radius50: half a letter. Every curve in the mark uses it, except the cup's belly (60) and handle (17).
Cut3 between solids that meet: the o's halves, the e's half-disc and quarter.
Hairline2.2 with round ends; 6 in the small-size cut.
Gaps12 between the columns and between the rows.
Legs20 wide, ending in half-disc drops. The m's arches stop exactly halfway.
Overall224 × 212. The saucer overhangs the left edge by 9 on purpose; align to the m, not the saucer.

The four letters

Each still reads as its letter.

Clear space and size

Two drips of air on every side.

Keep a clear space of 40 units around the mark: twice the width of one of the m's legs. Nothing else (type, edges, folds, other marks) goes inside it. The files ending in -clearspace include it.

Print12 mm wide minimum. Under 15 mm, use the small-size cut.
Screen40 px wide minimum for the full mark; 32 px and below, the small-size cut.
TinyBelow 24 px, use the monogram (the m alone).

The modes

One mark. Five modes, named.

The range is the modes. The mark is the same on every pack; the mode is named, never drawn into the letters.

Each mode has four cues: its name, set large; a line that says what it's for; a ground from the palette; and a thin band in its tint across the top, with the name in it. The band is the only place a mode tint appears, so the tints stay well under a tenth of any pack.

Names are always printed, so modes stay apart in one-colour print and for colour-blind customers. A sixth mode needs only a name, a line and a tint.

#SwitchYourMode is the sign-off: written as one word, used on the carton back, the mailer and social.

Lines, notes and caffeine values are placeholders for the brand team to confirm.

Magenta is the cut, cyan dashed the folds. Bleed 2 mm; keep text 3 mm inside every fold. Confirm the die with the converter before artwork goes final; a blank die is in packaging/carton/.

Mailer

The unboxing.

A kraft mailer printed one colour outside (the mark in Carbon, flexo) and flooded Roast inside the lid, so the first thing you see on opening is the four letters and the line that explains them.
